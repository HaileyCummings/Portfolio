Connect

Wire the model into the systems people already use. Most of the effort here is access, permissions, and plumbing, not AI.

  • Composio Gmail, Outlook, and Sheets connections (In my work)

Where the plan matters. Security review and data access are schedule risks. Name them in week one, with owners.

Build

Ship a first version that does one useful thing. Agents and chains come later, once the simple version has proved its worth.

  • Python my projects, end to end (In my work)

Where the plan matters. Scope the smallest slice that a real user would use, then protect it from additions.

Ground

Make answers come from the customer's own sources instead of the model's memory. Retrieval is where most enterprise assistants succeed or fail.

  • RAG retrieval-augmented generation (On my map)
  • Embeddings turn text into searchable vectors (On my map)
  • Chroma and FAISS local vector search (On my map)
  • pgvector and Pinecone vector search at scale (On my map)

Where the plan matters. Agree on the source of truth and who keeps it current. Stale documents make confident wrong answers.

Evaluate

Prove it works before anyone relies on it. A small test set and a clear pass mark beat a good demo.

  • Promptfoo and Ragas test prompts and retrieval (On my map)
  • LangSmith and Langfuse trace what the model did (On my map)
  • pytest test the code around the model (On my map)
  • Review rubric human acceptance checks (In my work)

Where the plan matters. Acceptance criteria, a go or no-go review, and a named owner for every failure.

A private place to document abuse, and a person to sit beside you in court

Next build. Shown as a case study for now: a concept, not a live service. Any demo uses made-up data.

For people leaving abusive situations who aren't ready to call the police. They log incidents with dates and times, assemble them into a packet for an investigator, get plain-language answers about the legal process, find free local support, and are matched with a trained volunteer to attend court with them.

  • Built for a shared or watched device. A quick-exit button, a neutral name and icon, and notifications that never reveal the purpose.
  • A log an investigator can trust. Entries are timestamped by the server, additions are allowed, and original entries keep their history.
  • A chatbot that stays in its lane. It explains the process from vetted sources, does not give legal advice, and surfaces hotline information whenever someone describes danger.
  • Volunteers as a safety program. Vetting, training, and a way to report problems come first, ideally with an existing advocacy organization.
  • Store less. Retention and what a court could compel are decided up front.
Proposed architecture
  1. Neutral appPlain name and icon, quick-exit button
  2. Private incident logServer timestamps, append-only history
  3. Evidence packetAssembled for an investigator
  4. Process assistantRetrieval over vetted sources, hotline shown when danger is described
  5. Court buddiesVetted, trained volunteers, matched by request

Field notes

Adoption is the deliverable. A tool that nobody changes a habit for is shelfware. ADKAR keeps the plan honest about awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, and reinforcement, in that order.

Ask what the requester sees. A self-service path beat a faster team, because it removed the waiting instead of speeding it up.

Make risk boring. When risks are listed, owned, and reviewed on a schedule, they stop being surprises and start being line items.
